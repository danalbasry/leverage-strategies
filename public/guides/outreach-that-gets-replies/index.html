Question 1 of 9

Does cold outreach still work for a small business?

Yes, when the list is small, the message is about the reader, and every fact in it is true. It does not work as a numbers game, and anyone who quotes you one "average reply rate" is hiding how they counted.

Published averages run from under 1% to over 4% because they count different things. Belkins found 0.45% across 7,530,489 emails in 2025, counting unique human replies per email sent (Belkins). Smartlead's median sender got 0.74% per contact across more than 850 million emails (Smartlead). Hunter puts its 2025 average at 4.5%, with sales outreach around 3% (Hunter). All three sell outreach tools or services, so these are vendor numbers, not a target I can promise you.

Two things in that data matter for a small business. Small companies answer more: Belkins measured 0.72% for companies with 0 to 10 employees. And the closest real example to this guide is on OpenAI's OpenWorks page. Ghost Grow 843, a microgreens grower in Charleston, South Carolina, used ChatGPT to "find nearby restaurants serving microgreens, identify chefs to contact, and draft emails in his own voice." The owner estimates his "response rate rose from 20% to 75%", and he gained 10 new restaurant customers in four months (OpenAI). That is one owner's estimate, not a benchmark. It does show the shape that works: a short local list, a real reason to write, and his own voice.

In Canada

Belkins' country split found 0.63% on 266,000 emails to Canada, against 0.51% on over 5.6 million to the US (Belkins). Canada's anti-spam law is much stricter than the US rules, though, so the list you may email is smaller. See the rules section.

Question 2 of 9

How do I find the right prospects with AI?

Build the list yourself from a real source, then use AI to research each business on it. AI is good at reading a website and summarising it. It is bad at producing the list.

Even Leadraft, which sells lead lists, says ChatGPT "can't give you a complete list of every business of a kind in an area" and "can't tell you whether an email address actually receives mail", and without search turned on "it may invent plausible ones." It adds that Google Maps stops a search after roughly 120 results and that Google's terms restrict scraping (Leadraft, vendor guide).

AI also gets local facts wrong. In an audit of 10,000 US local businesses, ChatGPT gave the wrong phone number 30.09% of the time and Perplexity 38.81% (Insites, a vendor-run test with a published method). BrightLocal found Yelp was a source in 80% of ChatGPT's local answers (BrightLocal, vendor-run test), so a list from one AI tool mostly reflects the directory it reads.

What I do:

  1. Write one sentence on who buys from you: type of business, distance, size, and the sign that they need you. A microgreens grower wants restaurants that already plate garnishes.
  2. Search Google Maps, your trade directory or your chamber's member list by hand, and copy 20 to 30 names and websites into a sheet.
  3. Ask AI to check each one against your list of signs, with a source link and a date for every fact, and "unknown" where it finds nothing.
  4. Open the links yourself for the ones you will contact. Delete anything closed, any chain, and any different business with the same name.

Question 4 of 9

How do I write a first message people answer?

Write about them, in your own words, with one true fact and one easy question. Keep it short: in Smartlead's data, first emails under 50 words earned a reply every 68 contacts, against every 97 contacts at 100 to 149 words (Smartlead, vendor data).

The rule that matters most with AI: it must never invent a fact. Give it only facts you checked, and tell it to say so when there isn't enough for a specific opening. Never let it state a hire, an award, a mutual contact, a result for another client, or what the reader "must be struggling with" unless you checked it yourself.

The prompt I use (copy it, fill the brackets):

Write a first email to [NAME], [ROLE] at [BUSINESS]. Use ONLY these facts: [YOUR CHECKED FACTS, WITH DATES]. About me: [ONE LINE ON WHAT I DO, FOR WHOM, AND WHERE]. Rules: under 80 words. The first sentence is about them. Ask one question they can answer yes or no. No compliments, no hype, no exclamation marks. If the facts are too thin for a specific opening, tell me instead of inventing one. End with my name, business, mailing address and this line: "This is a sales email. If you'd rather not hear from me, reply 'no thanks' and I won't write again."

The kind of line to watch for in any draft: "I loved your microgreen salad at the harvest festival." Nobody gave the AI that fact. If a draft has a line like it, cut it.

The message the owner approves, for the made-up Harbor Table:

Subject: greens for your winter tasting menu

Hi Chef [NAME],

I saw your new winter tasting menu on your website this month. I grow microgreens 6 km from you and deliver on Tuesdays and Fridays, cut the same morning.

Would a free sample tray of pea shoots and red amaranth help you test a dish? If yes, reply with a good day and I will drop it off.

[OWNER NAME], Little Field Greens (example business), [MAILING ADDRESS], [PHONE]

This is a sales email. If you'd rather not hear from me, reply "no thanks" and I won't write again.

The body is 61 words. Read it aloud before you send it. If it doesn't sound like you, rewrite the first line yourself.

What are the rules?

The US lets you email a business without asking first, as long as every message follows the rules. Canada needs consent before the first message, and you must be able to prove it. If you write to people in both countries, follow both.

In the US

the CAN-SPAM Act "makes no exception for business-to-business email." Every commercial email needs honest headers and subject lines, a clear statement that it is an ad, your valid postal address, and a way to opt out. You must honour an opt-out within 10 business days, and you stay responsible if someone else sends for you. Each email that breaks the law can cost up to US$53,088 (FTC). For Gmail, set up SPF or DKIM on your domain, and keep your spam rate below 0.3%, ideally below 0.1% (Google). And don't use AI voices for cold calls: the FCC confirmed that calls using AI-generated voices need the called party's prior express consent (FCC).

In Canada

Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL) needs three things in every commercial email, text or direct message: consent, identification and an unsubscribe (CRTC FAQ). For a cold first email the usual route is implied consent through "conspicuous publication": the person published the address, there is no statement with it saying they don't want commercial messages, and your message is relevant to their business role. Keep a screenshot or record with the date, the address and the page it was on. A list from someone else doesn't change this: as the sender, you "must still be able to prove" consent (CRTC guidance). Every message carries your name, a mailing address valid for at least 60 days, and an unsubscribe you act on within 10 business days. Penalties reach CA$1 million per violation for an individual and CA$10 million for a business. Direct messages in closed, two-way social media inboxes are covered. Phone calls are not CASL; they fall under the CRTC's telemarketing rules (CRTC FAQ). People do report: the CRTC's Spam Reporting Centre received 189,908 submissions from October 2025 to March 2026, and 93% cited lack of consent (CRTC).

In Quebec

the private-sector privacy law also covers the prospect details you keep, from collection to destruction (CAI). Keep only what you need, and store your prospect sheet securely.

This is general information, not legal advice. If outreach becomes a big part of how you sell, have a lawyer read your process once.

Question 6 of 9

How many follow-ups, and when do I stop?

I stop after two follow-ups, and each one must add something new: a photo of this week's harvest, a price list, or one direct question. Replies fall with each step. In Smartlead's data the first email in a sequence earned 1.18% and the seventh 0.33% (Smartlead, vendor data).

Stop at once on any reply, including "no" or "wrong person", on any unsubscribe, and on a bounce. I leave a few business days before the first follow-up and about a week before the second.

In Canada

each follow-up is a new commercial message, so it needs the same consent basis, identification and unsubscribe as the first (CRTC FAQ).

I send two follow-ups at most. Each one adds something new, like a photo or a price list. Replies drop with each email. In Smartlead's data, the seventh email got far fewer replies than the first (Smartlead).

Stop right away if someone replies, says no, unsubscribes, or the email bounces.

In Canada

every follow-up needs the same consent, details and unsubscribe as the first email (CRTC FAQ).

Send two follow-ups at most. Add something new each time. Stop if they reply or say no. Later emails get fewer replies (Smartlead).

Question 7 of 9

Where does an agent fit, and where do I approve?

An agent can do the research and drafting while you work: check each prospect's site, fill in your sheet, and line up drafts. It should not send. Sending is always your call, one message at a time.

  • ChatGPT Work (launched 9 Jul 2026): OpenAI says "You decide what it can access, when it should check in, and when it needs your approval before taking action" (OpenAI).
  • Claude with the Gmail connector can draft, send, reply and forward. By default, outside Auto mode, it asks for your approval before each of those. In Auto mode they can run without asking, and Anthropic warns "The check can miss risky actions" (Anthropic). Keep outreach on approval.
  • Claude in Chrome (paid plans) can browse prospect sites for you. Anthropic says to stay close for "messages sent as you", and Claude won't complete instructions it finds in emails or web pages (Anthropic).
  • Muse is free within usage limits, and its small business version connects to tools such as Shopify, QuickBooks, Stripe and Meta ad accounts (TechCrunch). Meta says it asks before sending an email (TechJournal).
  • HubSpot's Prospecting Agent, for businesses already on HubSpot Pro or Enterprise, costs US$1 per lead it recommends for outreach (HubSpot, vendor pricing).

Agents make the same mistakes as the chat tools above: old facts, the wrong business with a similar name, and a confident line with no source. They just make them faster, so the check before sending matters more, not less.
