Question 2

Which jobs are worth chaining, and which aren't?

Split a job only if you can say yes to three questions. Does it happen every week? Is it mostly finding, sorting or writing? Can you catch a mistake before a customer sees it? If any answer is no, do it by hand.

Never let a tool pay, sign or post by itself. You press that button.

Chain a job only if it passes three tests. It repeats on a schedule. Most of it is gathering, sorting or drafting. And you can catch a mistake before it leaves your business. If it fails one test, keep it manual, or chain only the prep work.

The more a customer depends on it, the slower you check. Anything that pays, signs or posts needs a person to press the button.

Work moves from tool to tool in five ways.

  1. Shared folder. One tool saves a file. The next one reads it.
  2. Connector. One tool logs in to another app.

  4. Automation. A tool like Zapier starts the next step for you.
  5. Copy and paste. You move it, after you read it. This is your check.

Question 5

Where does a person check, and what goes wrong at the hand-offs?

Check three times: before the first tool starts, between tools, and before anything goes out.

  • Copied mistakes. One tool's mistake gets passed along (OWASP).
  • Quiet changes. At one rental company, Google changed a form question. Budget answers stopped arriving. No alarm went off. Fewer than 10 leads came in before a person noticed (Zapier).
  • Hidden orders. An email can hide words that tell the AI what to do. One email could pull data out of Microsoft's Copilot (arXiv). Never follow orders found inside a file.
  • Doing it twice. Two automations can send the same email twice. Keep one timer per job.

Saying yes to everything does not help. People say yes to 93% of Claude Code's questions. Over time they stop looking closely (Anthropic). Check fewer things, but slowly.

In Canada

Air Canada had to pay about CA$812 because its chatbot gave a customer wrong information (summary). What your tools send is you talking.

Check in three places: the source before the first tool uses it, the file before the next tool reads it, and the last step before anything leaves.

Four things go wrong:

  • Copied mistakes. One tool's error becomes the next tool's fact. OWASP, a security group, calls this "cascading failures" (OWASP).
  • Quiet changes. At Panoramic Properties, Google changed a form question's wording. Budget answers stopped reaching the company's sales system, with no error. Fewer than 10 leads came in before a staff member caught it. He says: "I like to check everything manually 100% of the time." (Zapier's own story, Zapier.)
  • Hidden instructions. A document or email can hide text telling the AI to do something else. One crafted email could pull data out of Microsoft 365 Copilot with zero clicks (arXiv). Treat what a tool reads as information, never orders.
  • Doing it twice. Two automations on the same trigger send the same email twice. Keep one owner, one trigger, and one log.

Approving every step stops working. Anthropic says people accept 93% of Claude Code's permission prompts, and over time stop looking closely (Anthropic). Check fewer things, more carefully. Show the exact person, amount and date. Make "no answer" mean "don't send".

What rules apply when tools pass customer or staff data between them?

You are in charge of your customers' information at every step. Only pass on what the next tool needs. This is not legal advice.

In the US

AI can help write your reply to a real review. It must never make up a review or a customer (FTC). [CHECK YOUR STATE'S PRIVACY LAW]

In Canada

Tell people their information may be handled in another country (OPC). In Quebec, check the risks in writing and sign an agreement before data leaves the province (Law 25). Marketing emails need a yes from the person, your business name and a way to stop the emails (CRTC). Zapier keeps data in the US (Zapier).

You stay responsible for customer data at every step. Put only what the next tool needs into the chain. This is general information, not legal advice.

The FTC bans fake reviews. AI can draft your reply to a real review, but must never invent a customer or a review (FTC). Marketing email must follow CAN-SPAM. [CHECK YOUR STATE'S PRIVACY LAW]

In Canada

  • PIPEDA. You stay responsible for data you send to a tool. Tell people their data may be handled in another country (OPC).
  • Quebec. Before personal data leaves Quebec, do a privacy impact assessment and sign a written agreement (Law 25, s. 17).
  • CASL. Marketing email to anyone in Canada needs consent, your business name and a working unsubscribe, honoured within 10 business days (CRTC).
  • Where data sits. Zapier keeps data on US servers (Zapier). List each tool, what data it sees, and where it keeps it.

Every tool in the chain handles data for you, and you stay responsible for it at every step. Keep the minimum in the chain: send the review text, not the customer's whole order history. This is general information, not legal advice.

In the US

The FTC's rule on reviews bans fake consumer reviews, so AI can draft your own reply to a real review but must never invent a customer or a review. Your reply also can't falsely accuse the reviewer (FTC). Marketing email must follow CAN-SPAM, and [CHECK YOUR STATE'S PRIVACY LAW] before you pass customer data through a new tool.

In Canada

  • PIPEDA. Sending personal information to a tool for processing counts as a "use". You stay accountable, need a contract or other means for comparable protection, and must tell people in clear language that their information may be processed in another country (OPC guidelines).
  • Quebec. Before personal information leaves Quebec, you must do a privacy impact assessment and have a written agreement (Law 25, s. 17).
  • CASL. A marketing email to anyone in Canada needs consent, your business's identification and a working unsubscribe, honoured within 10 business days. This applies even if you send from outside Canada (CRTC). If your unsubscribe list lives in only one tool, every other tool in the chain must check it.
  • Where data sits. Zapier hosts data on servers in the United States and does not offer EU-only storage (Zapier). Write down, for each tool, what data it sees and where it stores it. That one page is the start of a Quebec assessment and your PIPEDA record.
