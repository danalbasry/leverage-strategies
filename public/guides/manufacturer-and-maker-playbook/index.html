Question 1 of 10

Which jobs is AI actually good at in a small shop?

AI is proven at office work first: writing, summarising, filling templates and doing arithmetic you can check. It is weakest where the answer depends on data it cannot see, like your rate card, your stock or your machine schedule.

The surveys agree on the shape, if not the numbers:

  • In the US Federal Reserve's 2025 survey of small employer firms, 46% said they use AI, but only 7% of users had fully built it into how they work. Accuracy was the top problem users named, at 46%. It is a convenience sample of 6,525 firms, not a random one. (Fed, 2026 Report on Employer Firms)
  • NIST's Manufacturing Extension Partnership reports that 46% of manufacturers use AI tools such as chatbots in operations, most often in production (39%) and inventory (33%). (NIST MEP, May 2026)
  • In Canada, Statistics Canada found manufacturers' planned AI use fell from 13.1% to 7.2% between Q3 2024 and Q3 2025, the largest drop of any industry. (Statistics Canada, Sep 2025)

So a lot of shops have tried a chatbot, and very few run a job on it. The useful move is to pick one repeated, text-heavy job and build it properly. For most shops that is the quote.

Question 2 of 10

How do I quote faster without letting AI set the price?

Give the AI one folder with everything a quote needs, tell it to use only those files, and check four things before you send. Prices come from your rate card, never from the AI's memory.

Step 1. Build a quoting folder (a Project). Put in it:

  1. Your rate card on one page: shop rate, setup charge, material markup, minimum order, rush charge.
  2. Your quote template with the fields every quote needs: scope, material, tolerances, finish, quantity breaks, lead time, validity date, exclusions, payment terms, tax line.
  3. Your standard terms.
  4. Three to five past quotes with customer names and prices removed (see the privacy section).
  5. A lead-time file a person updates every week.

Step 2. Write standing instructions. Paste this into the Project's instructions:

Price only from the rate card file. Never invent a material price, tolerance or lead time. Charge setup once per lot and run time per piece. Copy tolerances exactly as written on the drawing. If anything is missing, write MISSING and stop. Draft only. Never send.

Step 3. For each request, ask for the specs first, not a price. "List every specification in this request: material, size, quantity, finish, tolerances, delivery date. Quote the customer's words. Flag anything unclear."

Step 4. Then ask for the price build-up as a spreadsheet with formulas, one row per operation, with quantity breaks at 1, 10 and 100.

Step 5. Check, then send. Re-add the totals. Check the price at 1, 10 and 100 pieces. Check the tolerance against the drawing. Check the date against your shop board.

Question 3 of 10

How do I keep tolerances, specs and lead times right?

Copy, never infer. The AI copies the tolerance from the drawing, reads the lead time from your file, and flags anything it cannot find. A person compares the spec sheet with the drawing before it goes out.

Even tools built for this miss things. Paperless Parts says its Wingman 2.0 tool correctly identifies 88% of callouts on a drawing and correctly extracts the details of 83% (Paperless Parts, vendor claim). That is the vendor's own figure, and it still means roughly 1 callout in 6 needs a person. A general chatbot has no published score for this job at all. I found no independent test of AI on manufacturing quotes or spec sheets.

Rules I would set for any shop:

  • Tolerances: exactly as written on the drawing. If a dimension has none, apply the drawing's general tolerance note and say so on the quote. If there is no note, write MISSING.
  • Units: check them. A part drawn in inches and read as millimetres is a wrong quote.
  • Lead times: a date range with a start condition, such as "4 to 5 weeks from approved proof and deposit". Never a single number from memory.
  • Standards change. ISO 2768-2 (the geometric tolerance part) was withdrawn in 2021 and replaced by ISO 22081, and a revised ISO 2768 for sizes was announced for October 2026 (Makerverse). Ask your customer which edition their drawing means.

Question 4 of 10

Where does AI help with purchase orders, suppliers and shipping?

AI is good at the paperwork around buying and shipping: drafting purchase orders and supplier emails, comparing supplier quotes, and summarising tariff or origin rules for a person to check. It must not decide a tariff line, an origin claim or a delivery promise on its own.

Purchase orders and supplier quotes. Paste three supplier quotes into ChatGPT or Claude and ask for a side-by-side table: price, minimum order, lead time, freight terms, validity. Then ask it to draft the purchase order from your template. You check the part numbers and quantities against the quote you picked.

Supplier and buyer emails. Emails about an order you already have are routine. Sales emails to new buyers follow rules:

In the US

CAN-SPAM has no exception for business-to-business email. A sales email needs your postal address and a working opt-out, and opt-outs must be honoured within 10 business days. Penalties reach $53,088 per email (FTC).

In Canada

CASL generally needs consent before you send a commercial email. A purchase gives implied consent for two years and an inquiry for six months. Every message must identify you and carry an unsubscribe that works for at least 60 days, processed within 10 business days. Businesses can face penalties of up to $10 million per violation (CRTC). So an AI tool that blasts a bought list of Canadian buyers is the riskiest pattern there is.

Shipping across the border and tariffs. Tariffs now change quotes more often than material prices do, and the AI will not know this week's rate. As of the Government of Canada's Trade Commissioner page updated November 2, 2026: US Section 232 tariffs on steel, aluminium and copper run 10% to 50% on full value; US Section 338 tariffs of 50% apply to listed Canadian goods (including furniture, plastics, industrial machinery and wood products) with no CUSMA exemption; kitchen cabinets and vanities face 25%, rising to 50% on January 1, 2027; Canada's counter-tariffs started September 8, 2026, and the remission order runs to June 30, 2027 (Trade Commissioner Service). Use AI to draft the email asking a supplier for origin documents, or to summarise the remission rules, then check your own tariff line with a customs broker.

Labels and "Made in" claims. If AI writes your listing or packaging copy, check every origin claim yourself.

In the US

an unqualified "Made in USA" label needs final assembly in the US, all significant processing in the US, and all or virtually all parts made and sourced in the US (FTC).

In Canada

for non-food goods, "Product of Canada" generally needs at least 98% of direct costs in Canada; "Made in Canada" needs at least 51% plus a qualifier such as "with imported parts", and in both cases the last substantial change must happen in Canada (Competition Bureau). Food origin claims follow CFIA rules instead.

Question 5 of 10

Can AI write work instructions for the shop floor?

Yes, if you start from the real job and the person who does it reviews every step. Record your best operator talking through the job, turn the recording into text, ask the AI for numbered steps, then have that operator correct them and add hazards, PPE, settings and stop points. The AI cannot see torque values, hidden fasteners or the shortcut that causes injuries.

In ChatGPT

paste the transcript and photos and ask: "Turn this into a numbered work instruction. Keep every decision point as its own step and state the condition."

In Claude

the same prompt works with the transcript and photos. Transcribe audio first with your phone or another tool, then paste the text.

If you want the approved version at the machine, SOP365, a BC company, turns a phone video into a draft procedure, publishes it only after someone on your team approves it, and puts a QR code at the machine that opens the current approved version. It lists a free trial for 1 user, Team at C$49 a month for up to 10 users and Business at C$89 a month for up to 50 users (SOP365).

Translation: AI can translate steps for staff who read another language better. A bilingual worker checks the translation against the real task before it goes on the floor.

In the US

OSHA says an employer "must instruct its employees using both a language and vocabulary that the employees can understand", and handing reading material to workers who cannot read does not meet the duty (OSHA, 2010 policy). Specific hazards such as lockout and confined spaces carry their own written-procedure rules.

In Canada

safety law is provincial. In Ontario, an employer with more than 5 regular workers must have a written health and safety policy reviewed every year, and must instruct, inform and supervise workers (Ontario OHSA guide). In BC, a formal program with written instructions is required at 20 or more workers with a moderate or high hazard rating, or at 50 or more; smaller shops hold regular monthly safety meetings instead (WorkSafeBC). In every case the employer owns the procedure, not the AI tool.

Question 6 of 10

What could an agent do for my shop today, and where do I approve?

An agent can read a customer email, pull your rate card and past quotes, draft the quote and drop it in a review folder. It should not send, pay, order or promise a date. Agents are good at gathering and drafting and weak at finishing long jobs: in OSWorld 2.0, a research test from the academic XLANG Lab group with 108 real multi-step office workflows, the best system finished only 20.6% of tasks end to end, with a 54.8% partial score, even though the same model family scores about 83.5% on the original, shorter OSWorld tasks. Common failures were losing track of earlier constraints, guessing instead of asking, and not checking their own work (OSWorld 2.0, as reported by LLMs Blog).

A workflow across tools: customer email to draft quote to owner's review.

  1. A quote request arrives in Gmail or Outlook. Nothing has read it yet.
  2. An automation tool (for example Zapier) spots emails with "quote" in them and passes the email and attachments to ChatGPT or Claude with your Project's rules. Hand-off one.
  3. The AI returns a draft quote with every unconfirmed item marked UNVERIFIED and the lead time marked "check production calendar". The automation saves it to a "Quotes to review" folder and messages you. Hand-off two.
  4. You check price, stock and date (about 5 minutes on a simple job). Hand-off three, and the only one that matters.
  5. You send it yourself from your own inbox.

What must never go into an AI tool?

Customer drawings under an NDA that does not allow it, full recipes and formulas, supplier price lists marked confidential, anything export-controlled, and any work data on a personal account. A business plan is the minimum for real work.

  • Use a business plan. ChatGPT Business says it does not train on your business data by default (OpenAI). Anthropic says it does not train on inputs or outputs from its commercial products by default, unless you send feedback (Anthropic Privacy Center). On personal ChatGPT plans, the pricing page says content is used to train models unless you opt out (OpenAI), and Claude's personal plans are also opt-out (Anthropic).
  • Read the NDA before a customer's drawing goes in. A customer's drawing is usually their property. If the NDA is silent, ask in writing.
  • Strip names and prices from past quotes before they go into a Project: "Customer A", no addresses, no customer-specific discounts.
  • Controlled goods stay out. In Canada, you must register in the Controlled Goods Program before you even receive bid documents that contain controlled goods or technology. Registration is free, the standard processing time is 32 business days (currently longer), and penalties reach $2,000,000 and up to 10 years in prison (Government of Canada). In the US, drawings marked ITAR or EAR need an export-control adviser before any cloud tool touches them. Keep such files out of standard business AI plans.
