Question 1 of 5

Why should a prompt carry my own facts?

Because an AI with your price list, policy or notes in front of it works from them, and one without them guesses.

OpenAI's prompting guide describes adding your material as a way to keep the answer to the resources you chose (OpenAI).

Your facts cut errors. They don't remove them. In a Stanford study, legal research tools that searched a legal database before answering still got between 17% and 33% of answers wrong (Magesh et al., independent test, tools tested in 2024). So every prompt below has three parts: your facts, a rule to use only those facts, and an instruction to list what's missing instead of inventing it. You still read the result before it goes anywhere.

  2. A rule: use only these facts.
  3. An ask: tell me what is missing.

  1. 1

    Reply to a customer email

    You are replying to a customer for [BUSINESS NAME], a [TYPE OF BUSINESS] in [CITY].
Use only the facts below. If the answer is not in them, say so and tell me what to confirm.
Our facts: [HOURS, PRICES AND POLICIES THAT APPLY]
Customer's message: [PASTE MESSAGE]
Write a reply under 120 words in a [FRIENDLY / FORMAL] tone. End with one clear next step.
Then list anything you could not answer from my facts.

  2. 2

    Reply to a review

    Write a reply to this [STAR RATING]-star review for [BUSINESS NAME].
Review: [PASTE REVIEW]
What happened, from my side: [YOUR FACTS]
What I can offer: [OFFER, OR "NOTHING"]
Rules: thank them by first name only, no excuses, no promises beyond what is listed above, under 80 words. The reply is public, so do not confirm they are a customer or share any detail about their visit, order, health or account.

  3. 3

    Build a quote from my price list

    Build a quote for [CUSTOMER NAME OR TYPE] using only this price list: [PASTE PRICE LIST WITH UNITS]
Job details: [WHAT, HOW MUCH, WHERE, WHEN]
Tax: [YOUR SALES TAX NAME AND RATE]
Show each line with quantity, unit price and line total, then the subtotal, tax and total.
If the job needs anything that is not on my list, flag it instead of pricing it.

  4. 4

    Summarize last week's sales

    Here is last week's sales export from [POS OR TOOL]: [PASTE OR ATTACH FILE]
Here is a week to compare against: [PASTE, OR "NONE"]
Give me total sales, the top 5 and bottom 5 items, and any day well above or below the others.
Show the numbers in a table. Do not guess at causes. List questions I should check instead.

  5. 5

    Write this week's social posts

    Write 3 short [INSTAGRAM / FACEBOOK] posts for [BUSINESS NAME].
This week's specials, exactly as priced: [LIST]
Dates and hours: [DETAILS]
Our voice: [3 WORDS, FOR EXAMPLE WARM, LOCAL, PLAIN]
Use only these prices and dates. No claims like "best in town". One call to action per post.

  6. 6

    Turn notes into actions

    Here are my raw notes from a [CALL / MEETING] with [WHO] on [DATE]: [PASTE NOTES]
Turn them into three lists: decisions made, action items (each with an owner and due date), open questions.
If an owner or date is not in the notes, write "not stated". Do not add tasks that are not in the notes.

  7. 7

    Write an FAQ or policy page from my notes

    Turn these notes into a customer FAQ page for [BUSINESS NAME]: [PASTE NOTES ON BOOKINGS, REFUNDS, DELIVERY]
Write 6 to 10 questions customers really ask, each answered in plain words from my notes only.
Where my notes are unclear or silent, list the gap at the end instead of filling it.

  8. 8

    Write a job post

    Write a job post for a [ROLE] at [BUSINESS NAME] in [CITY].
Facts: pay [RANGE AND UNIT], hours [SCHEDULE], duties [LIST], must-haves [LIST], nice-to-haves [LIST], how to apply [STEPS].
Use only these facts. Plain words, under 300 words. Do not add perks, pay or requirements I did not list. Keep the wording neutral: nothing about age, gender, family status, health or other personal traits. If I give an AI use statement, include it word for word: [STATEMENT, OR "NONE"].

  9. 9

    Compare supplier quotes

    Compare these supplier quotes for [ITEM]: [PASTE EACH QUOTE WITH PRICE, UNIT, MINIMUM ORDER, DELIVERY FEE, TERMS]
Put them in one table on the same unit, delivery included. Mark the lowest cost per unit and anything a quote leaves out.
Do not look up or assume any price that is not in the quotes.

  10. 10

    Chase an overdue invoice

    Write a follow-up for an overdue invoice from [BUSINESS NAME].
Invoice: number [NUMBER], amount [AMOUNT AND CURRENCY], due [DATE], for [WORK DONE].
Our terms: [LATE FEE OR NONE, HOW TO PAY]
This is reminder number [1 / 2 / 3]. Under 100 words, firm but friendly. Do not threaten anything that is not in our terms.

What should never go into a saved prompt?

Passwords, card and bank numbers, ID numbers, health details and anyone else's private information.

Saved prompts get copied, shared and reused, so a secret in one travels with it every time. Google's Gemini privacy page asks people not to enter confidential information they wouldn't want a reviewer to see, and says a subset of chats are reviewed by humans (Google). Use slots like [CUSTOMER FIRST NAME], paste only what the job needs, and check your plan's data settings before any customer details go in.

When should a saved prompt become a job an agent runs?

When it uses the same kind of facts on a fixed schedule and nothing goes to a customer without you.

The weekly sales summary (prompt 4) and notes to actions (prompt 6) fit. The quote and the invoice chase don't run on their own, because each one changes and lands in front of a customer.
