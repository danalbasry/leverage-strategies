Question 1 of 8

Which jobs in a trades business is AI actually good at today?

AI is good at the office side of a trades business: drafting quotes from prices you give it, turning talk into written notes, and writing customer replies and follow-ups. It is weak at the field side, and it has no business deciding code, permit or safety questions.

The surveys agree on the office jobs, even though they disagree on how many shops use AI at all. In Jobber's survey of 1,050 US home service owners (December 2025), 52% said they use AI, and among those users 54% use it for estimates, quotes or contracts, 52% for invoices and 51% for emails, proposals and job descriptions (Jobber, vendor survey). ServiceTitan's survey of 1,017 contractors (the post doesn't name a country) found 61% use at least one AI tool, from 72% in commercial landscaping down to 37% in pest control, and about six in ten users report little or no trust in it (ServiceTitan, vendor survey). Both are vendor surveys of people already using software, so they run high.

In Canada

Statistics Canada's official survey found 19.2% of businesses used AI to produce goods or deliver services in the 12 months to Q2 2026, up from 12.2% a year earlier. Construction was among the lowest at 9.2% (Statistics Canada, June 11, 2026). No Canadian survey I found breaks this down by trade, so the trade-by-trade numbers above come from vendor surveys, not Canadian data.

Question 2 of 8

How do I turn my price list into quotes with AI?

Put your price list, your rules and your quote template in one saved project, tell the AI to use only those files and to flag anything missing, then check every line before the quote leaves your hands.

The setup is the same idea in every tool:

  1. One clean price list file. One row per item: code, description, unit, price. Put the date in the file name (for example price-list-2026-11.xlsx) and replace the file the day a price changes. A photo of a whiteboard is the worst input; retype it.
  2. Written rules. Service call fee, labour rates, minimums, what tax applies where, payment terms, what you don't do.
  3. The instruction that matters most: "Use only prices from the price list file. If an item is not on the list, write NOT IN PRICE LIST and do not price it. Never invent a part, a price, a fee, a warranty or a date. Show quantity × unit price = line total for every line. Put tax on its own line for the province or state I name. Mark every quote DRAFT."
  4. A person checks and sends from your quoting or invoicing tool, not from the chat window.
The instruction to paste
Use only prices from the price list file. If an item is not on the list, write NOT IN PRICE LIST and do not price it. Never invent a part, a price, a fee, a warranty or a date. Show quantity × unit price = line total for every line. Put tax on its own line for the province or state I name. Mark every quote DRAFT.

The quantity trap is the one that hurts after signing. The customer asked for six lights, but if the four-light quote of $1,406.85 had gone out as an Ontario estimate, the real job at $1,779.75 is 26.5% over it. Unless she agreed to the new price, you could charge at most $1,547.54 (110% of the estimate).

In the US

Rules are set by each state. New York, for example, requires a signed written home improvement contract with the price, a description of the work and materials, any progress payment schedule in dollars and cents, and the owner's right to cancel until midnight of the third business day (NY General Business Law §771). A bare AI total is not that contract. Sales tax on trades work also depends on your state, so write your state's rule (from your accountant) into the project rules and never let the AI guess a rate.

In Canada

In Ontario, if an estimate is part of a home renovation contract, the final price can't be more than 10% over it unless the customer agrees to new work or a new price, and any home renovation contract over $50 must be in writing (Ontario). In Alberta it is an unfair practice to charge more than 10%, to a maximum of $100, over an estimate, unless the customer consents or asks for more work; a court read the $100 as a ceiling, so on the Fernhill job, a four-light Alberta estimate of $1,307.25 could not be exceeded by $100 or more without her agreement, while the real job is $346.50 more (Legal 500 on Patel v WG Housing). In Quebec, a fixed price in a contract can't be raised later because the work cost more (Civil Code art. 2109), electricians and plumbers can't invoice more than agreed or bill unauthorised work (OPC), and your RBQ licence number must be on every estimate, bid, contract and invoice (RBQ). Put that number in the template, not in the prompt.

One more reason the check matters in both countries: in Moffatt v. Air Canada, a BC tribunal held the airline responsible for what its website chatbot told a customer, saying the company "is responsible for all the information on its website" (Legal 500). It is one tribunal decision, not binding everywhere, but the lesson travels: what your AI sends is yours.

Question 3 of 8

Can I talk instead of type?

Yes. Every major AI app takes voice now, and your phone's own keyboard microphone types into any app, so you can talk the job notes in the truck and let AI turn them into a quote draft.

There are two different things, and it helps to know which you want:

  • Dictation turns your speech into text you can read and fix before sending. Best for job notes and quote details.
  • Voice mode is a spoken back-and-forth. Best for thinking out loud ("what did I forget to ask?") while driving or with your hands full.

In ChatGPT

Tap the voice icon in the message bar to talk, or the microphone to dictate. Voice is listed on every plan, Free included, with higher limits on paid plans (ChatGPT pricing). Start the voice chat inside your quotes project so it has your price list.

In Claude

In the mobile app, tap the sound-wave icon next to the microphone for voice mode, or the microphone for dictation. Voice mode is on all plans, Free included (beta). Anthropic says hands-free works best in quiet places and suggests push-to-talk in noisy ones. By default the audio is deleted after it is turned into text (Anthropic).

How accurate is it? On a clean phone line, the best transcription models got about 94 to 95 words in 100 right in a November 2026 test run by Stork.AI, an AI tools directory that sells listings, so not fully independent (word error rate 5.5% to 6.0%, and the gaps between the top models were inside the margin of error), and names were the hardest thing for every model (Stork.AI). That test used role-played calls, a simulated phone line and no trade vocabulary, so expect worse with a saw running or a strong accent. Practical habits: say numbers and quantities slowly, spell names and street names, say corrections clearly ("change that to six"), and read the transcript before it becomes a quote. In the Fernhill example, the correction from four lights to six is exactly the kind of thing to check.

In the US

Federal law allows recording a call if one party consents, but 11 states require everyone's consent by one current count, including California, Florida, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Pennsylvania and Washington (Legal Desire, citing RCFP and Justia, updated November 17, 2026). A voice note to yourself is not a recording of anyone else. If a customer is on the call, say "this call is recorded" at the start.

In Canada

The Criminal Code allows recording a conversation when one party consents (Justice Laws, s. 184), but privacy law adds more for a business. The Privacy Commissioner's guidance, updated October 8, 2026, says to tell the customer the call is recorded, say why, get consent, and offer a choice if recording isn't essential (OPC).

Question 6 of 8

What must stay private and safe?

Keep customer access details out of AI tools, use a business plan or turn training off, and never let AI answer a code, permit, gas or electrical safety question for you.

  • Never paste or say: lockbox, alarm or garage codes; card or bank details; a customer's address together with when the home is empty; photos of the inside of a home unless the customer agreed. A quote doesn't need any of these. Canada's privacy regulators say to use de-identified data rather than personal information where personal information isn't needed (OPC, December 7, 2023).
  • Business plan vs personal account: OpenAI says it doesn't train on ChatGPT Business data by default (OpenAI). On Claude's consumer plans, training depends on your Model Improvement setting (Anthropic). On the consumer Gemini app, with history on, Google can use chats to train models and a subset is read by human reviewers (Google).
  • Your field-service tool counts too: Housecall Pro's terms say "Your User Data may be used to train and enhance our proprietary AI models," and that you are solely responsible for checking AI outputs, including estimates (Housecall Pro terms). Read your own tool's terms.
  • Safety: Dakota Prep, an exam-prep company, reported that ChatGPT got more than 60 of 100 National Electrical Code questions wrong in its own internal test, which hasn't been independently repeated (AI Home Building). Use AI to find what to look up, never for the verdict.

In the US

Code questions go to your licensed electrician, plumber or gas fitter and the local inspector. An AI answer is not a permit, not an inspection and not a defence.

In Canada

In Ontario, only Licensed Electrical Contractors (or electricians they employ) can be hired for electrical work, and the contractor files the notification of work with the ESA (ESA). Only TSSA-registered fuel contractors and certified gas technicians may work on gas appliances (TSSA, March 25, 2026). If you serve Quebec customers, ask a privacy adviser about Quebec's privacy law (Law 25) before customer details go into any AI tool.
