Question 1 of 13

What AI is inside QuickBooks today?

QuickBooks Online now has a set of named AI helpers under one name, Intuit Intelligence. Most of them suggest and draft. The one that acts on its own is the categorising of your bank transactions, and you cannot switch that off.

Intuit's help centre lists eight: Accounting AI ("done for you" bookkeeping), Payments AI (invoices and getting paid), Finance AI (a summary of how the business is doing), Customer AI (leads and follow-ups), Project Management AI, Payroll AI, Sales Tax AI (checks your sales tax before you file) and Business Tax AI (possible deductions). Intuit says each can "act on your behalf with your permission", and also that "there isn't a way to turn off AI features individually" (Intuit help).

The chat is limited. Only primary and company admins can use Intuit Intelligence, the monthly prompts are capped, an add-on costs US$10 a month for more, and "there are currently no plans that offer unlimited prompts" (Intuit help).

Intuit's headline number is that businesses using its AI invoice reminders "get paid 4 days sooner on average". That is a vendor claim: its own footnote says it compares US customers using AI notifications with customers sending standard reminders, March 2025 to March 2026 (Intuit). It is not an independent test, and I found none for any QuickBooks AI feature.

Question 2 of 13

What AI is inside Xero today?

Xero's AI is called JAX (Just Ask Xero). It does two different jobs, and they behave differently: a chat you open and ask, and automatic bank reconciliation that matches bank lines without asking you first.

JAX chat. It is "available to all Xero subscribers and users", there is "currently no additional charge", and Xero reserves the right to change that pricing later. It only sees what your Xero permissions allow, and it "won't run in the background unless you ask a question". It can answer questions about your numbers, research questions such as "What's the average revenue for a restaurant with five employees?", and draft an invoice from the last quote you sent a customer (Xero Canada, JAX). Xero still labels JAX beta (Xero, JAX).

Automatic bank reconciliation. This is the part that acts on its own. When new bank lines arrive, JAX reconciles a line only "if there's high confidence", and labels how it decided (Xero Canada):

Label on the lineWhat it meansHow hard to look
MatchMatched to an invoice, bill or payment already in XeroCheck the amount and the invoice
RuleOne of your own bank rules appliedCheck the rule once, then it repeats
MemoryCopied how you reconciled similar lines beforeCheck if your past coding was right
PredictionCopied how other Xero users reconciled similar linesLook hardest: it is not based on your books

Lines it is not sure about wait for you, with a suggestion. You can review everything it did on a "Review reconciled" screen, edit the account in the row, or use "Remove & Redo" to reverse a line. Each correction teaches it, separately for each bank account (Xero Canada).

Xero's own figures are that it "auto-reconciles over 60% of the transactions it sees, with ~96% accuracy". Read the footnote: accuracy means "the percentage of auto-reconciled lines not corrected by users", April to July 2026 (Xero Canada). A mistake nobody noticed counts as accurate. That is a vendor claim, not a test.

Smart Document Capture reads receipts and bills you photograph, email or drag in, and pre-fills a record for you to approve. It is included on every plan (Xero Canada pricing).

In the US

When you review an auto-reconciled line, "tax rate editing is currently not available in the US" (Xero). Xero Payroll, powered by Gusto, is a US add-on (Xero US pricing).

In Canada

The JAX, reconciliation and document capture pages above are Xero's Canadian pages, so these features are offered to Canadian subscribers. You can edit the GST/HST rate on a reconciled line in the review screen.

Xero's AI is called JAX. It does two jobs: a chat you can ask questions, and automatic bank matching that works without asking you.

The chat. Every Xero user can use it, and right now it costs nothing extra. It only works when you open it and ask. It can answer questions about your numbers and draft an invoice from your last quote (Xero Canada).

Automatic bank matching. When a new bank line comes in, JAX matches it only if it is very sure. Each matched line gets a label:

Document reading. Take a photo of a receipt or email a bill, and Xero fills in a draft for you to approve. It is on every plan.

Question 4 of 13

What does each one get wrong?

Both get the easy, repeating lines right most of the time and struggle with the same few kinds of transaction: money moving between your own accounts, owner money, loan payments, part payments and anything new. Neither vendor publishes an independent accuracy test, so the best evidence is bookkeepers who tried them on real files.

QuickBooks. A bookkeeper who tested the AI bank feeds in 2025 found Google G Suite coded correctly as Software & Apps, then suggested as "Memberships & Subscriptions" on the next charge. A vendor that sells training and conferences went to Legal & Accounting Services because its name contained "accountant". Amazon purchases she kept coding to Office Supplies were still suggested as "Supplies". When unsure, it fell back to General Business Expenses or an income account. She went back to the classic bank feed (5 Minute Bookkeeping, Aug 2025; the firm sells bookkeeping training). A US cleanup firm reported in August 2026 that "owner draws and contributions get coded as expenses or income", loan payments get "principal and interest lumped together", transfers between your own accounts get booked as income on one side and expense on the other, and the AI "scales the existing errors instead of fixing them" because it learns from your file's history (QBO Cleanups; the firm sells cleanup work).

Xero. An Australian bookkeeping firm, Digit, ran auto-reconciliation on client files from January 2026 for up to ten weeks, with 18 staff responses. On simple files it reconciled 69 of 116 lines in 30 days for a day spa and 26 of 39 for a web developer, with no errors flagged. It failed on transfers between related accounts ("It's not reliable in this regard"), could not reconcile part payments "even though the invoice number and customer name" were in the description, struggled with batch deposits, coded a GST-free subscription with GST, and coded one subscription right the first time and wrong the second. Their advice: turn it on per bank account, for example the credit card, not the account you move money through (Digit, Mar 2026; Digit sells bookkeeping). Cam Lynch of Vibe CFO turned it on in July 2026 and found it had reconciled about 200 of about 300 lines, posting payments "without matching them to invoices", including foreign-currency invoices and one a cent out, so those invoices still showed unpaid, and mixing up credit card transfer dates (The AI Exhale, episode 20, 25 Sep 2026; Vibe CFO sells an AI finance product).

The general AI models underneath. On APEX-Accounting, 160 month-end close tasks written by 42 accounting professionals, the best model met 56.4% of the grading criteria, and "58% of tasks were never fully solved by any model on any run". The common failure was reasoning, not finding the data: models "find a discrepancy and then drop or contradict it" (APEX-Accounting summary). Mercor built it with Ramp, which sells finance software, so read it as a careful vendor-backed test. It did not test QuickBooks' or Xero's own AI.

Sales tax is where a quiet error costs most, and the two countries differ.

In the US

QuickBooks' pre-filing sales tax check is on Plus and above ("Catch discrepancies pre-filing with Sales Tax AI", QuickBooks US pricing). It is a check on your data, not advice on what is taxable in each state you sell into.

In Canada

An Ottawa CPA firm warns that GST/HST in QuickBooks is two systems, not one: the books and the sales tax return "do NOT automatically stay in sync", "journal entries don't fix GST returns", and "GST/HST adjustments inside QuickBooks do NOT affect your accounting records" (Dexado, 8 Oct 2026). QuickBooks' Sales Tax AI, on EasyStart and above in Canada, is the right kind of check: it compares income on your Profit and Loss with net sales on the return and lists what causes the gap. But it "does not make changes for you", it is beta with limited availability, and it runs only on the web version (QuickBooks Canada help).

The pattern across all of it: the errors that look fine are the dangerous ones. A total that is a few percent off does not make anyone look twice. The next section shows how that happens on one month of books.

Both do well with simple, repeating lines. Both make mistakes with the same kinds of lines: money moved between your own accounts, money the owner takes out, loan payments, part payments and new suppliers.

QuickBooks. One bookkeeper found it put the same Google charge in two different categories, a month apart. It guessed a company was an accounting firm because "accountant" was in its name. It kept getting Amazon purchases wrong after she fixed them (5 Minute Bookkeeping). A cleanup firm says it codes owner money as an expense, lumps loan payments together, and repeats old mistakes from your file (QBO Cleanups).

Xero. A bookkeeping firm tested it on client files for up to ten weeks. On simple files it matched about 6 in 10 lines with no errors found. It got transfers between accounts wrong, could not handle part payments, and coded one bill right once and wrong the next time (Digit). Another bookkeeper found it had matched about 200 of about 300 lines, but some payments were not linked to their invoices, so those invoices still showed as unpaid (The AI Exhale).

The AI models underneath. In a test of 160 month-end tasks, more than half were never fully solved by any model (APEX-Accounting).

In the US

QuickBooks' sales tax check before you file is on the Plus plan and up.

In Canada

A CPA firm warns that in QuickBooks, your books and your GST/HST return are two separate things. Fixing one does not fix the other (Dexado). QuickBooks' Sales Tax AI compares the two and lists what is different. It does not fix anything for you.

Both do well with bills that are the same every month. Both make mistakes with:

  • money moved between your own accounts
  • money the owner takes out
  • loan payments
  • bills paid in parts
  • new suppliers

Bookkeepers who tested them found these mistakes (Digit, QBO Cleanups).

Question 6 of 13

How do they handle sales from my till or online shop?

Use the shop's connector, not the AI, to get sales in. A good connector records each sale, the fees and the tax separately and then matches the payout to the bank. The AI only works on bank lines after that, so if the sales arrive as one net deposit, the AI will happily code the whole deposit as one thing.

QuickBooks with Shopify. Intuit's Shopify Connector creates "a Sales Receipt (for sale details) and an Expense (for fees)" for each confirmed sale and holds them in Undeposited Funds. When you confirm the payout it creates a deposit that moves the net amount to your bank account. A setting called "Add transactions automatically" lets QuickBooks add high-confidence transactions without asking; turn it off and they wait for review. Intuit's rule: confirm transactions in the app transactions tab first, and in the bank feed "use Match", never Add, for the deposit, because adding it by hand would record the money a second time (Intuit help).

Xero with Shopify. Sales "flow automatically to Xero each day", as a daily summary or as individual sales, and payouts from Shopify Payments and other gateways are matched to the bank with fees, refunds and taxes handled. The integration is free, with a 30-day trial (Xero Canada).

In Canada

Where does an agent fit, and where do I approve?

Let an agent do the repetitive middle: matching, sorting, drafting and listing what needs you. Keep four things in your own hands: anything that pays money, anything a customer receives, anything filed with the IRS, a state or the CRA, and any change to a period you have already closed.

What the vendors themselves say they will not do without you:

  • QuickBooks: every action is "reviewable, reversible, and yours to approve" (Intuit). The exception is categorising: confident lines are marked ready to post, and you post them in bulk, so a bulk click is an approval you should make on purpose.
  • Xero: JAX chat waits to be asked; automatic reconciliation acts alone on high-confidence lines and logs the method on each one; you review and reverse with Remove & Redo (Xero Canada).
  • Claude with Xero: read-only, so it can list and explain but not change anything (Xero Canada).
  • Claude or ChatGPT with QuickBooks (US): can create and send invoices; a new invoice is saved at once, so check it before it is sent (Intuit help).

What agents still get wrong is what the testers above found, only faster: transfers, owner money, part payments and new vendors. An agent that is right most of the time still needs a person who checks the exceptions.

Question 10 of 13

What should I still check myself?

Check the exceptions and the confident lines, not everything. The AI hands you its unsure lines; the risk is in the lines it was sure about. How much checking you need depends on your books: a business with one card account and repeating suppliers needs far less than one with loans, several accounts and online sales.

Before you turn anything on:

  1. Clean up the chart of accounts, because the AI learns from your past coding and repeats it.
  2. Write a bank rule for every transfer you make between your own accounts and for owner draws.
  3. In Xero, turn auto-reconcile on for simple accounts first, such as a credit card, and leave the account you move money through for later (Digit's advice above).
  4. Decide an amount above which you look at every line yourself.

Every week (about 15 minutes):

  1. Read what was posted or auto-reconciled, largest first.
  2. Look at every transfer, owner draw, loan payment and anything in a catch-all account.
  3. In Xero, filter to Prediction lines and check each one.

Every month:

  1. Reconcile every bank, card and loan account to its statement balance. A duplicate or a missing payout shows up here.
  2. Compare your till, POS or shop sales report with the deposits, so fees, refunds and payouts in transit are accounted for.
  3. Compare each expense category with last month. A wrong rule shows up as a sudden jump.

Before you file sales tax:

  1. Tie the tax collected in your books to the tax on your sales reports.
  2. In QuickBooks, run the Sales Tax AI pre-file check where you have it, and fix the transactions it lists.
  3. In Canada: remember the QuickBooks books and the GST/HST return do not fix each other; correct both.

Check the lines the AI was sure about, not only the ones it asks you about. How much you check depends on your business. One card and the same suppliers every month means less checking. Loans, many accounts and online sales mean more.

Before you turn it on: tidy your list of accounts, and set a rule for every transfer and every owner payment. In Xero, start auto-matching on a simple account like a credit card.

Every week (15 minutes): read what was posted, biggest first. Look at every transfer, owner payment and loan payment. In Xero, check every Prediction line.

Every month: match each bank, card and loan account to its statement. Compare your till or shop sales report with your deposits. Compare each cost with last month.

Before you file sales tax: make sure the tax in your books matches the tax on your sales reports. In Canada, remember that in QuickBooks the books and the GST/HST return do not fix each other.

Each week: look at the biggest lines, and every transfer, owner payment and loan payment.

Each month: match your books to your bank statement. Compare your till report with your deposits.
