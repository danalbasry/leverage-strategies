Question 1 of 10

What can AI on your phone do well, and what should wait?

Your phone is great for catching work the moment it happens, and weak for checking it.

Your phone is great for catching work as it happens. It is weak for checking it.

The phone is good for catching work. It is not good for checking it.

Speak a note in the truck, snap a receipt at the counter, ask a quick question while you stand in front of the job. Then check anything with a number, a name, a date or a price on a bigger screen, or slowly on the phone, before it reaches a customer or your books.

That split is the whole article. How much you lean on the phone depends on three things:

  • Your job. A painter quoting on site needs voice and camera. A bookkeeper needs a desk most of the day.
  • How costly a mistake is. A wrong grocery list costs nothing. A wrong total on a quote costs money and trust.
  • Who sees the result. Anything a customer sees, anything that moves money, and anything you post gets a human check first. Every time.

The five set-ups below are the jobs where the phone earns its place. Each one shows the steps in the apps that do it well, and where you must check.

Set-up 2: How do I turn a photo of a receipt into a line for my books?

Take a clear photo. Ask for the date, store and total. Tell it to write UNCLEAR, not guess. Check the total on the paper.

The output is a draft row, not a posted entry. Faded receipts are where every app is most likely to get a number wrong, and the wrong answer usually looks just as neat as a right one.
