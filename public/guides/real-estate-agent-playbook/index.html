What do fair housing and advertising rules say about AI copy?

The rules do not care who or what wrote the copy. They care what the ad says to an ordinary reader, and they hold you, the licensee, responsible for it.

The rules look at what the ad says, not who wrote it. You are responsible for it.

The rules look at what the ad says. You are in charge of it, not the AI.

In the US

  • The law. The Fair Housing Act makes it unlawful to publish any notice, statement or ad for the sale or rental of a dwelling that indicates a preference, limitation or discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status or national origin (42 U.S.C. 3604(c)). HUD's rule covers words, phrases, photos and symbols, and also choosing media or locations that keep groups from seeing the ad (24 CFR 100.75).
  • HUD's 2024 guidance on housing ads on digital platforms (ad targeting and delivery) is withdrawn. HUD withdrew it effective 17 Sep 2025 and says it "should not be relied upon as authoritative", while actions that break the Act "continue to be subject to enforcement" (Federal Register notice FR-6571-N-01, published 6 Apr 2026). Many courses and vendor pages still cite it as HUD's AI guidance, though the notice does not mention AI. The law it interpreted has not changed.
  • NAR. NAR's Code of Ethics, which binds REALTORS (members), also covers this. Articles 2 and 12 require you to avoid exaggeration and misrepresentation and to present a true picture in advertising. NAR's broker AI policy template lists phrases like "perfect for young families" and "quiet, mature community", and references to nearby houses of worship, as examples to avoid, and says agents are "fully accountable for any AI-generated content they use, share or publish" (NAR, 23 Oct 2026).
  • States. California's regulator says responsibility for misleading AI advertising "rests with the licensee and their responsible broker, not the technology provider", and since 1 Jan 2026 California requires a disclosure on digitally altered listing images plus access to the original (California DRE advisory, 17 Mar 2026). Wisconsin's photo rule starts 1 Jan 2027 (NAR). Check your own state and MLS. Many states and cities also protect more groups than federal law, such as source of income or age, so check those rules too.

How do I handle follow-ups without breaking consent rules?

Let AI draft the message, but decide yourself who it goes to and how, because consent rules depend on the channel and the country. A follow-up that sells something is a commercial message in both countries.

Let AI write the message. You decide who gets it and how.

AI can write the message. You choose who gets it.

In the US

  • Texts and calls. Marketing calls to cell phones made with an autodialer or an artificial or prerecorded voice need prior express written consent: a signed agreement, electronic signature allowed, that names the number and says consent is not a condition of buying (47 CFR 64.1200). The same rule bars telephone solicitations before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. in the recipient's local time. A phone number on a website inquiry is not written consent to marketing texts. Many states add their own rules.
  • Email. CAN-SPAM does not require consent before sending, but every commercial email needs honest headers and subject lines, a clear opt-out, a valid postal address, and opt-outs honoured within 10 business days. Each email in violation can cost up to US$53,088 (FTC).

Do Not Call. The national Do Not Call rules cover sales calls and text messages to cell phones, however they are dialled. You cannot make a telephone solicitation to a number on the national registry unless you have the person's signed permission or an established business relationship, which lasts 3 months after an inquiry and 18 months after a purchase or transaction (47 CFR 64.1200).

  • Marketing texts or calls sent with an autodialer need signed written consent first. No calls before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. their time (47 CFR 64.1200).
  • Marketing emails need an opt-out, your postal address and honest subject lines. Opt-outs must be honoured within 10 business days (FTC).

Do Not Call: sales calls and texts to numbers on the national Do Not Call list are not allowed, however you dial, unless the person gave signed permission or you have a recent business relationship. That lasts 3 months after they ask about a home and 18 months after a deal (47 CFR 64.1200).

  • You need signed permission before sending marketing texts with auto-dialing tools (47 CFR 64.1200).
  • Marketing emails need a way to say "stop" and your mailing address (FTC).

Sales calls and texts to numbers on the Do Not Call list are not allowed unless the person said yes in writing, or asked about a home in the last 3 months, or did a deal with you in the last 18 months (47 CFR 64.1200).

In Canada

  • CASL covers email and text the same way. You need consent, identification and a working unsubscribe (CRTC CASL FAQ). Implied consent lasts six months after an inquiry and two years after a transaction. Unsubscribes must be honoured without delay and within 10 business days.
  • The clock matters. In my made-up example, a buyer who asked about the Hamilton semi on 1 June 2026 is covered by implied consent until 1 Dec 2026. A client who closed on 16 March 2026 is covered until 16 March 2028. Ask for express consent before the clock runs out, and record when and how they said yes.

Calls are different. CASL does not cover live or automated telemarketing calls; those follow the CRTC's Unsolicited Telecommunications Rules (CRTC CASL FAQ). Every telemarketer must register with the National Do Not Call List, must not call numbers on it unless the call is exempt, must use a version of the list no older than 31 days, and must keep its own internal do-not-call list (CRTC).

  • CASL treats texts and emails the same. You need consent, your contact details and an unsubscribe (CRTC).
  • Implied consent lasts 6 months after someone asks about a home, and 2 years after a deal. In my example, an inquiry on 1 June 2026 is covered until 1 Dec 2026.

Calls are different: CASL does not cover sales calls (CRTC). Sales calls follow the CRTC's telemarketing rules. You must register with the National Do Not Call List and not call numbers on it unless the call is exempt (CRTC).

  • You need permission before you send marketing texts or emails (CRTC).
  • If someone asked about a home, you have permission for 6 months. After they buy or sell with you, you have 2 years.

Phone calls have their own rules. Sign up with the National Do Not Call List. Do not call numbers on it, unless the rules say your kind of call is allowed (CRTC).
