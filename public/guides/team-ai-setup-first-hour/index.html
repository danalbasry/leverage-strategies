Question 1 of 8

Do we need a team plan, or are personal accounts enough?

If two or more people use AI for company work, and any of that work touches customers, staff or money, a team plan is worth it. A solo owner can manage with a personal plan and good habits.

Here is what a team plan changes:

  • Training is off by default. OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Microsoft each say they don't train their models on business-plan data by default (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft). On personal accounts, each person has to find and change the setting themselves.
  • The company owns the account. When someone leaves, you remove them in one place. On personal accounts, their chats about your customers leave with them.
  • You set the rules once. Sign-in, sharing, memory and connections to your other apps are set by an admin, not by each person.

Two honest catches:

  • Your admins can see more. OpenAI's privacy page says ChatGPT Business admins "can view, access, export, and delete" the conversations in the workspace (OpenAI). In Claude Team, the Primary Owner can request an export of the organization's data (Anthropic). Microsoft admins can find Copilot chats with Content search or Microsoft Purview (Microsoft). Tell staff plainly: work chats are company records.
  • Off by default isn't off for everything. Some settings still start on, and the first hour is mostly about those.

The U.S. Small Business Administration's advice is short and still right: "Try not to feed any sensitive data or proprietary information," and make sure "a person assesses all the messages and outreach campaigns that AI generates" (SBA, updated February 14, 2025). A team plan makes the first part easier. It doesn't do the second part for you.

Question 3 of 8

Who should be able to do what?

Give admin power to two people, no more, and keep everyone else as a regular member. Two, so one can fix things if the other is away or locked out. Google requires at least one super admin and recommends at least two (Google).

Each tool names the roles differently:

In ChatGPT

ChatGPT Business has Owner, Admin and Member roles (OpenAI). Single sign-on (SSO, signing in with your company's Google or Microsoft account) is included (OpenAI). OpenAI's own admin guide, updated September 17, 2026, says that in Business, Members can invite other members and "any user can remove other users from the workspace" (OpenAI Academy). Check your Members page to see who can remove whom in your workspace today. The same guide says to verify your company email domain, set up SSO early and plan how people leave.

In Claude

Claude Team has four roles: User, Admin, Owner and Primary Owner. Admins can invite and remove members. Only Owners can change roles, see billing, turn on integrations and turn on project sharing. Only the one Primary Owner can request data exports (Anthropic). Two invite settings start on for Team: invite links and member-to-member invites. Turn them off if you want every new person to go through an admin (Organization settings > Organization and access) (Anthropic).

In Gemini

In Google Workspace, people get Gemini through their Workspace account. A super admin can do everything. A User Management Admin can add and remove regular users but can't touch other admins (Google). You choose who gets the Gemini app in Admin console > Menu > Generative AI > Gemini app > Service status, for everyone or by department (organizational unit) or group (Google).

In Copilot

In Microsoft 365, Copilot follows the person's licence. Copilot only shows a person data they can already view, so your SharePoint and Teams permissions are the real controls (Microsoft). Fix "everyone can see this folder" problems before you switch Copilot on.

When someone leaves, removing them doesn't always cut the bill. In Claude Team, removing a member "frees up their seat for reassignment, but does not automatically reduce your plan's total seat count" (Anthropic). Their private projects become unreachable to everyone else, so ask people to share anything the business needs before their last day (Anthropic). Check the same two things in whichever tool you use: does the seat stop billing, and where does their work go?

Question 4 of 8

Which defaults should I change in the first hour?

Four kinds of setting matter on day one: training, memory, sharing and how long chats are kept. Training is already off on all four business plans. Memory and sharing are the two that most often start on.

Training. Off by default on all four (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft). One exception to know: in Claude, if someone presses thumbs up or thumbs down on an answer, Anthropic may use that conversation to train, and keeps feedback for up to 5 years (Anthropic).

Memory is when the AI remembers things about a person from past chats. Useful for one person, risky when that person pastes a customer's details on a Tuesday and gets them back in an unrelated chat on Friday.

Sharing is who can open a chat or project someone else made.

Retention is how long chats are kept before they're deleted.

In ChatGPT

  1. Training: off by default on Business (OpenAI).
  2. Memory: workspace owners can turn memory off for the whole workspace. Turning it off deletes members' saved memories. Each person can also turn it off in their own Settings (OpenAI Help Center).
  3. Shared links: a shared chat opens only for members of your workspace. Business has no switch to turn sharing off for everyone; that's Enterprise. Each person deletes their links in Settings > Data controls > Shared links > Manage (OpenAI Help Center).
  4. Retention: OpenAI's privacy page says Business admins "can control how long your data is retained," and deleted chats are removed within 30 days, with exceptions (OpenAI). But its pricing page lists custom retention under Enterprise (OpenAI). If you don't see the setting, make deleting old chats a habit.

In Claude

  1. Rate chats: turn it off in Organization settings > Data and Privacy. This removes thumbs up and down for everyone and closes the feedback exception (Anthropic).
  2. Memory: in organizations created on or after October 31, 2026, memory "starts on for each member." In older organizations it's off until each person turns it on (Anthropic). An Owner can turn it off for everyone in Organization settings > Capabilities. That keeps existing memories rather than deleting them; each person can reset their own in Settings > Your Claude (same page).
  3. Sharing: "Share projects" and "Public projects" both start on, in Organization settings > Data and privacy > Sharing. With Public projects on, everyone in the organization can find and chat in a public project. Turn Public projects off if client work should be invite-only (Anthropic).
  4. Retention: Team chats are kept until deleted; deleted chats leave Anthropic's systems within 30 days (Anthropic). I found no retention setting for Team, so deleting old chats is a habit, not a switch.

In Gemini

  1. Conversation history: on by default and kept 18 months. Change it in Admin console > Menu > Generative AI > Gemini app > Gemini conversation history: 3, 18 or 36 months, or off (Google).
  2. Memory: Google's Personal Intelligence feature isn't available on Workspace accounts (Google). The bigger risk is staff using a personal Google account's Gemini for work. The policy below rules that out.
  3. Sharing: when a file is shared outside your company, Gemini's side panel shows an out-of-domain warning (Google). It's a warning, not a block.

In Copilot

  1. Memory: "By default, Enhanced personalization is turned on." An admin can only turn it off through a Microsoft Graph setting (enhancedPersonalizationSetting), not a switch in the admin center. Purview retention doesn't apply to Copilot memory, memory actions aren't logged, and admins "can't restrict what type of information is added" (Microsoft). If your team handles sensitive customer information, ask your IT person to turn it off, or tell staff to clear Settings > Personalization.
  2. Sharing: Copilot session and response sharing "is enabled by default." Links open for anyone in your organization. Turn it off in Microsoft 365 admin center > Copilot > Settings > Copilot session and response sharing, by clearing "Allow users to share Copilot sessions and responses." It's all or nothing for the whole company (Microsoft message center notice MC1461700). A shared chat doesn't give anyone access to the files it mentions (Microsoft).
  3. Retention: set it with a Microsoft Purview retention policy for Copilot interactions (Microsoft).
  4. Other AI models: Microsoft lets admins decide whether to use Anthropic and OpenAI models inside Copilot, with extra terms (same page). Leave them off unless you have a reason.

Question 5 of 8

Where should shared work live?

Put your business's standing facts in one shared space in your tool, and keep each person's day-to-day chats private. That way the whole team works from the same prices, hours and policies, without seeing each other's customer conversations.

What goes in the shared space: your services and prices, hours, service area, refund and booking policies, the tone you want, and two or three examples of replies you were happy with. What stays out: customer names and contact details, staff records, anything under a confidentiality agreement.

In ChatGPT

Make a project, add the files and instructions, then share it. On business plans, projects can only be shared with members of your workspace. Each person gets chat access or edit access, and each shared project has its own private memory (OpenAI). Unlike Claude, the chats aren't private: people with chat access can "see and interact with the project's chats," and can branch a teammate's conversation (OpenAI Help Center). Everyone in a shared ChatGPT project can see the chats inside it. Use it for drafting from your facts only, and never put customer details in it.

In Claude

Make a project and choose who can see it: invited people only, or everyone in the organization. Inside a shared project, each person's chats stay private unless they share one (Anthropic). One catch: Google Drive files can only be added to private projects, so upload copies of your fact files to a shared project and update them when prices change (Anthropic).

In Gemini

Use a skill: saved instructions Gemini can use in Gmail, Docs, Slides, Drive and Chat. Skills started reaching Workspace accounts on November 9, 2026 for Rapid Release domains and from November 30, 2026 for Scheduled Release domains, so yours may not have them yet. They aren't in Sheets or Meet yet, they're on by default, and they "currently support English prompts and instructions only" (Google). Gems still work on Workspace accounts for now (same page).

Question 6 of 8

What should it be allowed to connect to, and what about agents?

Start with nothing connected, then add one connection at a time, read-only first, for the people who need it. A connection (a "connector" or "app") lets the AI read your email, files or other software, and sometimes act in them. The AI can then reach everything that person can reach.

That matters because of one real risk. Security researchers at Radware showed that a single email with instructions hidden in white text could make ChatGPT's research agent, connected to Gmail, send inbox data to an attacker. No click was needed and nothing showed on screen. OpenAI fixed it in August 2025 (Radware). That flaw is fixed, but the lesson isn't tool-specific: anything the AI reads can carry instructions, so give it the least access that does the job.

In ChatGPT

ChatGPT Business includes connections (OpenAI calls them apps) to Google Workspace, Slack, GitHub, Microsoft 365 and more (OpenAI). "In ChatGPT Business, apps are enabled by default." Question 7 of 8

What goes in a one-page AI policy?

Nine short rules: which tools, what never goes in, what's fine to use, who checks, what AI never decides, reviews, agents, mistakes and leavers. Keep it to one page so people read it, and have everyone sign it.

Copy this, fill in the brackets, and change anything that doesn't fit:

One-page AI policy · template

[BUSINESS NAME] AI rules

Owner of these rules: [NAME]. Start date: [DATE]. Next review: [DATE, 3 MONTHS FROM NOW].

  1. Use only our approved tools, signed in with your work account: [TOOL AND PLAN]. No personal or free accounts for company work. Want a new tool? Ask [NAME].
  2. Never paste anything that identifies a customer or staff member: names with addresses, phones or emails; card, bank or payment details; door, alarm or lockbox codes; passwords; pay, health or disciplinary records; anything under a confidentiality agreement. Not sure? Don't paste it, and ask.
  3. Fine to use: public information, our prices and policies, your own drafts, examples with names removed.
  4. AI writes drafts. A person checks every fact, price, date and promise before anything goes to a customer, supplier or the public.
  5. AI never decides about people. No screening job applicants, and no decisions about pay, hours or discipline.
  6. No fake reviews. Never post or request reviews written by AI.
  7. Agents ask first. Any AI that can send, share, delete, book, pay or post must ask a person before it does.
  8. Made a mistake? Tell [NAME] the same day. An honest mistake reported the same day won't count against you. Hiding one will.
  9. When someone leaves, we remove their AI access that day and move any shared work they own.

I've read these rules and I'll follow them. Name: Date:

This is a starting point, not legal advice.

Why each rule is there:

  • Rule 4 is the SBA's advice that a person should assess everything AI generates (SBA).
  • Rule 6: the FTC's rule bans reviews "that misrepresent that they are by someone who does not exist, such as AI-generated fake reviews" (FTC, August 14, 2024).
  • Rule 8: people hide mistakes they'll be punished for. You want to hear about a pasted customer list the same day, while you can still delete it.

In the US

The SBA notes there are "no federal or state laws that require businesses to disclose the use of AI," but calls disclosure "an expected best practice" (SBA). That page was last updated in February 2025, and some states now have rules on AI in hiring. Rule 5 keeps AI out of hiring decisions. It isn't legal advice, so check your state's rules. What does the first hour look like for a small team?

Six blocks of about ten minutes each. Here they are for a made-up 11-person plumbing firm. The settings and prices are real; the business isn't.

Made-up example business

Quillbrook Pipe & Heat

11 people: the owner, an office manager, a part-time bookkeeper and 8 technicians. Already on Google Workspace Business Standard. The office writes quotes, answers customer emails and books jobs. The owner also wants ChatGPT Business for the office manager and himself. That's a second tool, added on purpose for two people only, and reviewed after three months.

Minutes 0 to 10: plan and seats. Gemini is already included in their Workspace plan, so all 11 people have it at no extra cost (11 × US$14 = US$154 a month paid yearly, or CA$202.40 in Canada, which they were paying anyway) (Google). ChatGPT Business needs at least 2 seats: 2 × US$25 = US$50 a month paid monthly, which they pick while they test it (US$40 a month paid yearly) (OpenAI). An independently owned US business can also check whether it qualifies for OpenAI's OpenWorks program, which includes 3 months of ChatGPT Business at no cost (OpenAI).

Minutes 10 to 20: people and roles. The owner is Google super admin and the ChatGPT workspace Owner. The office manager becomes the second admin in both (a second super admin in Google, as Google recommends, and Admin in ChatGPT). They agree that only the owner changes data settings. Technicians are regular Google users and aren't added to ChatGPT. Because any ChatGPT Business member can invite and remove people, they write "only the owner adds people" into the policy.

Minutes 20 to 35: data defaults. In ChatGPT: the owner turns memory off for the workspace for now (nobody has saved memories yet, so nothing is lost) and they agree to revisit it in a month. Shared links can't be switched off on Business, so the policy says to share chats only with each other. In Google: conversation history set from 18 months to 3 months.

Minutes 35 to 45: connections. In Google, they look at the Marketplace apps list and turn off the seven third-party connectors, since they don't use any of those apps. In ChatGPT, apps start on: in Admin Console > Plugins they turn off everything they don't use, and under Workspace settings > General > Plugin permissions they choose "Always ask."

Minutes 45 to 55: shared work. They upload the fact sheet the office manager wrote the day before: services, call-out fee, hourly rates, service area, hours, warranty and cancellation policy. They put it in a shared ChatGPT project with the instructions above. Everyone in a shared ChatGPT project can see its chats, so they use it for drafting from the fact sheet only. They save a Gemini skill with the same instructions, if their domain has skills yet.

Minutes 55 to 60: the policy. The owner fills in the one-page policy, prints it, and puts "sign the AI rules" on Monday's team meeting agenda.
