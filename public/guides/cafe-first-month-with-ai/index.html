Step 3 of 11

Weeks 1 to 2: Which three jobs does the café pick first?

Laneway picks social posts and the specials board, supplier order lists, and the weekly sales check. Reviews and the schedule wait for round two, and two jobs fail the safety gate.

I score each job the way I describe in How I work with a small business. Hours per week times how easy it is to check (3 easy, 2 medium, 1 hard), and then a safety gate. A job fails the gate if the AI's answer would reach a customer with no one reading it, if it moves money or changes records, or if it touches health information.

Step 4 of 11

Weeks 2 to 3: What gets built?

For each of the three jobs I build one small saved tool and test it on the café's own work: its menu, its count sheet, its sales file. Nothing new is bought apart from AI seats for the two people who use the tools.

1. Posts and specials board. A saved assistant (a Project, in both ChatGPT and Claude) holds the café's menu, prices, opening hours, ten past posts the owner liked, and the rules: no allergen claims, no "best in Toronto", no food photos the café didn't take. The owner gives it the special and the photo and gets back a post and a board line to edit.

2. Supplier order lists. A second Project holds the list of every item, its supplier and its usual order size. The shift lead photographs the paper count sheet at close, and the assistant returns a checklist grouped by supplier. The owner checks it against the shelf before ordering. Cole Morgan built his version as a custom GPT. I wouldn't today: OpenAI has scheduled custom GPTs to stop running on December 11, 2026 (OpenAI), so I build it as a Project instead.

3. The weekly sales check. Each Monday the owner exports last week's sales and asks the same set of questions, using the same prompt each time. There are two ways to do it.

In Square

Square now has its own assistant inside the Dashboard, so you can ask without exporting anything.

In the US it's called Squaremates. Sign in to Square Dashboard, where it opens as your landing page, and type your question in the box (Square Help). Square says it made it available to US sellers on November 12, 2026, at no additional cost (Square).

In Canada it's called Square AI. Square says it launched on March 9, 2026, at no additional cost, and answers questions like "Which items sell best on weekends?" (Square Canada). One Toronto café manager quoted by Square said: "Now I just ask a question and it builds the table for me." That's Square's own press release, so read it as a vendor story.

  1. Export last week's sales from your till. Daily totals are enough to start. What does the sales file actually say?

The file says Laneway's month was shaped by four things: weekends, rain, a week of road work and one seasonal drink. Here is what I get when I work the numbers myself, in code, so you have an answer key to check any AI's answer against.

Weekends carry the café. On a normal dry day with nothing unusual in the notes, a weekday took in $1,197.63 on average (about 132 transactions) and a weekend day took in $2,033.88 (about 190). That's about 70% more per day at weekends. People also spent more per visit at weekends: $10.71 against $9.08.

Rain hurt every day it fell. Weekdays give the cleanest comparison: the four rainy weekdays averaged $951.09, about 21% below a normal dry weekday. The two rainy Saturdays were lower too, but there are only two normal Saturdays to compare them with, so I wouldn't put a number on it.

The road work cost roughly $1,900. From October 13 to 17 the notes say "road work out front". Those five days took in $4,977.82, about $1,900 less than the same days of the week took in on normal dry days. Treat that as rough: each day of the week has only two or three normal days to compare with. The weekdays that week ran about 26% below a normal weekday.

The lowest day had a reason. Thanksgiving Monday (October 12) took in $513.59 from 56 transactions, with short hours.

Pumpkin spice took over. The file names one top seller per day. Before October 19, pumpkin spice latte topped 2 of 18 days. From October 19 it topped 11 of the last 13. The best week was October 19 to 25 at $9,967.20, and the best day was Sunday October 25 at $2,338.01.

Three things Laneway could try next week. These are tests, not promises. Each one has a way to tell whether it worked.

  1. Try a lighter rain-day prep. Check the forecast on Sunday. On a weekday with rain forecast, prep fewer sandwiches. If you want to try lighter staffing too, set it when you post the schedule, as a trial, and note it in the file. Check: compare what's left at close, and any sell-outs, against a dry weekday.
  2. Protect the weekend. Weekends bring about 70% more per day. Make sure the best staff and full prep are on Saturday and Sunday mornings. Check: count sell-outs and the longest line on the next two weekends.
  3. Decide what replaces pumpkin spice. It drove late October, and it's seasonal. Pick the next special now. Check: its daily count against the oat latte's, using the item report, since the daily file only names one top seller.

Step 6 of 11

How do you check what the AI tells you?

You check it against numbers you trust, because AI tools still get spreadsheet work wrong. In a benchmark posted in November 2026 (a preprint, not yet peer-reviewed), researchers gave AI models 70 realistic workplace spreadsheet requests, and the best one passed only 38.3% of its attempts (GDP.xlsx, arXiv). Square says the same about its own assistant: "they can make mistakes. Please validate Squaremates' responses for accuracy" (Square Help).

Here is the trap I see most with this file. A quick average of all six rain days against all the dry days says rain cost about 15%. That's wrong, because two of the rain days were Saturdays and the dry days include Thanksgiving and the road work week. Compare rainy weekdays with normal dry weekdays and it's about 21%. Both answers look reasonable. Only one compares like with like.

Four checks I teach every owner:

  • Does the total match the till? Ask for the month's total and transaction count first. For this file it's $40,056.74 from 4,110. If that's wrong, nothing after it counts.
  • Show me the rows. Ask which days it used for each claim. If it can't list them, don't use the claim.
  • Like with like. Weekdays against weekdays, and leave out days with a note (holidays, road work).
  • What can't the file answer? If it gives you profit or staffing advice from a file with no costs or hours, it's guessing.

Step 7 of 11

Weeks 3 to 4: How do the staff use it day to day?

The staff use two of the three tools every day, with one page of rules and one go-to person. At Laneway that's the shift lead who already does the count sheet.

The training is short and on real work, as in How I work with a small business: one session where each person does a real task start to finish, one where we look at the tool's mistakes on purpose, and a check-in after I've gone. Training is what operators say they're missing. In the National Restaurant Association's survey released in November 2026, training and education topped the list of support that would help them adopt these tools, named by 56.5% (National Restaurant Association).

Laneway's one page of rules:

  • Use the café's own paid account only, never personal accounts.
  • Never paste customer names, emails, phone numbers, card details or anyone's allergy notes.
  • AI never writes an allergen line from scratch. It may only copy from the signed-off allergen list, and a person checks it against the recipe.
  • Never post a food photo the café didn't take of food it actually serves.
  • A person reads every post, order list and review reply before it goes anywhere.
  • Report a mistake the same day. No one gets in trouble for reporting.

Why the allergen rule differs by country. The lists aren't the same.

In the US

The nine major food allergens are milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans and sesame (FDA). What a café must tell customers about allergens in food it plates depends on its state or county. The FDA's Food Code is a model code that each state adopts in its own way: as of December 31, 2025, 14 states had adopted the newest (2022) version, covering 25.6% of the population (FDA). Ask your local health department.

In Canada

The priority allergens are eggs, milk, mustard, peanuts, crustaceans and molluscs, fish, sesame seeds, soy, sulphites, tree nuts, wheat and triticale, plus gluten sources (CFIA). Mustard, sulphites and molluscs aren't on the US list, so an AI tool that has learned the US list can miss them. These labelling rules are for prepackaged food. For food you plate, ask your local public health unit.

In Quebec

Menus must be in French. Another language can sit beside it, but not more prominently (Charter of the French Language, s. 51). AI tools write good French, but they won't apply that rule unless you tell them. Write the French first and have a French speaker check it, on the menu, the board and the posts.
